In [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science "Computer science"), an **invariant** is a [logical assertion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_assertion "Logical assertion") that is always held to be true during a certain phase of execution of a [computer program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_program "Computer program"). For example, a [loop invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loop_invariant "Loop invariant") is a condition that is true at the beginning and the end of every iteration of a loop.

Invariants are especially useful when reasoning about the [correctness of a computer program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Correctness_(computer_science) "Correctness (computer science)"). The theory of [optimizing compilers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimizing_compiler "Optimizing compiler"), the methodology of [design by contract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Design_by_contract "Design by contract"), and [formal methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_methods "Formal methods") for determining [program correctness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Program_correctness "Program correctness"), all rely heavily on invariants.

Programmers often use [assertions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assertion_(computing) "Assertion (computing)") in their code to make invariants explicit.
